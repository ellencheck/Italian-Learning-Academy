A1 · GRAMMAR COURSE · LESSON 03

ESSERE — to be

Learn how to introduce yourself, talk about where you are from, describe people and places, and say how you feel. We will build this step by step, with examples and practice.

12–18 minutesBeginner A1Grammar + speaking
Lesson contents1. Meaning & uses2. Six forms3. Subject pronouns4. Model sentences5. Gender & number6. Common mistakes7. Practice · 10 questions8. Make it personal

01 · UNDERSTAND THE VERB

What is essere?

Essere is one of the two most important verbs in Italian. Its basic English meaning is “to be.” We use it to connect a person or thing with information: who they are, what they are like, where they are, and how they feel.

Identity · Chi?

Who is this person? Name, identity or role.

Sono Anna. Lui è Paolo.

I am Anna. He is Paolo.

Origin · Di dove?

Where are you or someone else from?

Siamo di Roma.

We are from Rome.

Location · Dove?

Where is someone or something right now?

Il libro è sul tavolo.

The book is on the table.

Feelings & descriptions

How do people feel? What are they like?

Oggi sono stanco.

Today I am tired.

Important — essere is not always followed by a noun.

You can use it with an adjective (sono felice), a place (sono a casa), or a description (il mare è calmo).

02 · MEMORIZE THE FORMS

The six forms of essere

Italian verbs change form according to the subject. Learn this table aloud: pronoun → verb → English meaning.

SubjectEssereMeaning
iosonoI am
tuseiyou are · one person, informal
luièhe is
leièshe is
noisiamowe are
voisieteyou are · plural
lorosonothey are

Say the pattern out loud, twice: sono · sei · è · siamo · siete · sono.

Two forms look the same.

Io sono means “I am”; loro sono means “they are.” The subject or context tells you which person you mean.

03 · WHO IS DOING THE ACTION?

Subject pronouns

The subject pronoun identifies who we are talking about. In Italian, it is often omitted because the verb ending makes the subject clear. Keep the pronoun when you want to emphasize or contrast.

Sono italiana.
I am Italian. (The word io is understood.)
Io sono italiana, ma lui è italiano.
I am Italian, but he is Italian.
Here the pronouns help contrast two people.
Tu sei pronto? Io sono pronta.
Are you ready? I am ready.

For formal “you,” Italian uses Lei with the same verb form as lei (she): Lei è pronto/a? In this beginner lesson, focus first on tu and voi.

04 · READ AND NOTICE

Everyday sentences

Read each Italian sentence, then its English meaning. Notice which form of essere is used.

Mi chiamo Lucia. Sono di Firenze.
My name is Lucia. I am from Florence.
Sono → io (I), understood.
Tu sei molto gentile.
You are very kind.
Sei → tu (you, singular).
Marco è in ufficio.
Marco is in the office.
È → lui (he).
Mia sorella è felice.
My sister is happy.
È → lei (she).
Siamo a casa oggi.
We are at home today.
Siamo → noi (we).
Voi siete in ritardo!
You are late! (talking to several people)
Siete → voi (you plural).
I bambini sono stanchi.
The children are tired.
Sono → loro (they).
Il caffè è caldo, ma l'acqua è fredda.
The coffee is hot, but the water is cold.
È is used with each singular noun.
Anna e Paolo sono amici.
Anna and Paolo are friends.
Sono → two people together (plural).

05 · GRAMMAR DETAIL

Adjectives change, too

When essere is followed by an adjective, the adjective usually agrees with the person or thing described. Many adjectives have four forms.

Person / groupExampleTranslation
Masculine singularMarco è stanco.Marco is tired.
Feminine singularAnna è stanca.Anna is tired.
Masculine plural / mixed groupMarco e Luca sono stanchi.Marco and Luca are tired.
Feminine pluralAnna e Lucia sono stanche.Anna and Lucia are tired.

More examples: contento/contenta (happy), pronto/pronta (ready), italiano/italiana (Italian), occupato/occupata (busy).

Age is different in Italian!

English says “I am 25,” but Italian says Ho 25 anni (“I have 25 years”). Age uses avere, not essere. You will learn avere in another lesson.

06 · COMMON CONFUSIONS

Watch out for these

❌ Tu sono pronto. → ✓ Tu sei pronto.
Use sei with tu, not sono.
❌ Anna e felice. → ✓ Anna è felice.
È with an accent means “is”; e without an accent means “and.”
❌ Noi è amici. → ✓ Noi siamo amici.
Use siamo with noi.
❌ Io 25 anni. → ✓ Ho 25 anni.
Age uses avere. (This is a preview of the next grammar topic.)

07 · PRACTICE

10 quick questions

Choose one answer for each sentence. Correct answers are locked in and your feedback appears immediately. Incorrect answers can be retried.

1. Io ___ di Napoli.

2. Voi ___ molto gentili.

3. Giulia ___ a scuola.

4. Noi ___ pronti.

5. I miei amici ___ italiani.

6. Tu ___ stanco?

7. “E” or “è”? Anna ___ Maria sono sorelle.

8. ___ vent'anni. (I am 20 years old.)

9. Anna e Lucia sono ___. (tired)

10. Io e Paolo ___ in centro.

08 · PERSONAL SPEAKING

Make the language yours

Now write—and say—your own sentences. Use the patterns below, but change the information to make it true for you.

Sono ________. · I am ________.
Name, profession, or a description.
Sono di ________. · I am from ________.
A city or country.
Oggi sono ________. · Today I am ________.
Choose a feeling or adjective.
La mia città è ________. · My city is ________.
Describe a place with one adjective.

Read them aloud three times. First slowly, then naturally, then without looking at the screen.

LESSON RECAP

You learned essere.

Remember the six forms: sono · sei · è · siamo · siete · sono. Use them to describe identity, origin, location, feelings and characteristics. Adjectives agree with the person or group you describe.
