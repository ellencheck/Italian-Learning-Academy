GRAMMAR · A1 BEGINNER

ESSERE — to be

Meet one of the most important verbs in Italian. Use essere to say who you are, where you come from, what you do, and how you feel.

◷ 12–15 min✦ 4 learning goalsBeginner · A1
In this lessonWhen to use essereAll six formsIn real sentencesCommon mistakesQuick practiceYour turn

01 · THE IDEA

What does essere do?

Essere means to be. In Italian, it connects a person or thing to information about them. It is not a “helper” you can skip: the verb changes depending on who you are talking about.

Identity

Say your name or who someone is.

Sono Alina.
I am Alina.

Origin

Say where someone is from. Use di with a city or country.

Sono di Kyiv.
I am from Kyiv.

Profession

Talk about work or what you do.

Sono insegnante.
I am a teacher.

Feelings & description

Describe a feeling, condition, or characteristic.

Siamo felici.
We are happy.

02 · CONJUGATION

Six forms to learn

The subject tells you which form of essere to use. In everyday Italian, the subject pronoun is often dropped because the verb ending already identifies the person.

PersonEssereEnglish
iosonoI am
tuseiyou are (singular, informal)
lui / leièhe / she is
noisiamowe are
voisieteyou are (plural)
lorosonothey are
Notice the accent

È (he/she is) has a grave accent. E without an accent means “and”: Anna e Marco — Anna and Marco.

Subject pronouns are optional
Io sono stanca. → Sono stanca.
Both mean “I am tired.”
But the verb stays
Say Sono felice, not just felice, when you mean “I am happy.”

03 · IN CONTEXT

Hear it in everyday Italian

Read the complete phrase first. Then notice how the form of essere matches the subject.

Tu sei molto gentile.sei → tu
You are very kind.
Lui è al lavoro.è → lui
He is at work.
Noi siamo in Italia.siamo → noi
We are in Italy.
Voi siete pronti?siete → voi
Are you ready? (plural)
Loro sono amici.sono → loro
They are friends.
Sono a casa.subject “io” is understood
I am at home.

04 · WATCH OUT

Three mistakes to avoid

Match the subject

❌ Tu sono stanco.
✓ Tu sei stanco.
“Tu” goes with “sei.”

Don't confuse è and e

❌ Anna e felice.
✓ Anna è felice.
“è” means “is.”

Don't leave essere out

❌ Io felice.
✓ Io sono felice.
Italian still needs the verb.

Use the right “you”

Tu sei = one person (informal).
Voi siete = several people.
Formal “you” is Lei è.

05 · CHECK YOURSELF

Choose the correct form

One question at a time. Pick your answer and check the feedback.

1. Io ___ una studentessa.

2. Anna ___ italiana.

3. Voi ___ pronti?

4. Noi ___ a Roma.

06 · MAKE IT YOURS

Say something about yourself

Complete these three sentences out loud. Then write them down so you can remember your own examples. Use the reveal buttons if you need a model.

1. Say your name: Io sono…

Io sono Alina. — I am Alina.

2. Say where you are from: Sono di…

Sono di Kyiv. — I am from Kyiv.

3. Say how you feel today: Oggi sono…

Oggi sono felice. — Today I am happy.

Read your sentences aloud twice. The goal is to say them smoothly—not to memorize a grammar chart.

LESSON SUMMARY

Now you can use essere.

Identify the subject, choose its matching form, and use the verb to talk about yourself and other people. Come back to this page any time you need a refresher.
