A1 · LESSON 08

Family & Describing People

Family vocabulary, possessive adjectives, appearance and personality

Beginner · A160–75 minutesExplanations · Examples · Practice
In this lesson
1. Learning goals2. Family vocabulary3. Possessive adjectives4. Describing appearance5. Personality6. Talking about age and relationships7. Examples in context8. Practice and answer key9. Speaking task
PART 01

Learning goals

Talk about your family, introduce relatives, describe appearance and personality, and use possessive adjectives correctly with family nouns.

PART 02

Family vocabulary

la famiglia (family), i genitori (parents), la madre / la mamma (mother / mum), il padre / il papà (father / dad), il fratello (brother), la sorella (sister), il figlio / la figlia (son / daughter), i figli (children), il marito (husband), la moglie (wife), il nonno / la nonna (grandfather / grandmother), i nonni (grandparents), lo zio / la zia (uncle / aunt), il cugino / la cugina (cousin), il nipote / la nipote (nephew/niece or grandson/granddaughter, depending on context), il compagno / la compagna (partner).

PART 03

Possessive adjectives

mio/mia/miei/mie (my), tuo/tua/tuoi/tue (your informal), suo/sua/suoi/sue (his/her/your formal), nostro/nostra/nostri/nostre (our), vostro/vostra/vostri/vostre (your plural), loro (their). The possessive agrees with the thing possessed, not the owner: mio fratello, mia sorella, i miei genitori, le mie cugine. Usually use an article: la mia casa, il tuo libro. With a singular close family member, the article is normally omitted: mia madre, tuo fratello, suo marito. The article returns with plural family members (i miei fratelli), modified nouns (la mia cara sorella), and loro (la loro madre). Diminutives may behave differently.

PART 04

Describing appearance

alto/a (tall), basso/a (short), giovane (young), anziano/a (elderly), magro/a (slim), robusto/a (solid build), capelli lunghi/corti/ricci/lisci (long/short/curly/straight hair), occhi azzurri/verdi/marroni (blue/green/brown eyes), barba (beard), baffi (moustache). Use avere for features: Ha gli occhi verdi; Ha i capelli ricci. Use essere for general description: È alto e magro.

PART 05

Personality

simpatico/a (pleasant/friendly), gentile (kind), tranquillo/a (calm), allegro/a (cheerful), timido/a (shy), generoso/a (generous), intelligente (intelligent), divertente (funny/fun), serio/a (serious), curioso/a (curious), paziente (patient), socievole (sociable). Adjectives agree in gender and number: Marco è simpatico; Anna è simpatica; Marco e Luca sono simpatici; Anna e Sofia sono simpatiche. Adjectives ending in -e have the same singular form for masculine and feminine, and usually -i in the plural.

PART 06

Talking about age and relationships

Use avere for age: Mia sorella ha vent'anni. Use essere for identity and relationship: Marco è mio fratello. Use chiamarsi to say what someone is called: Mia nonna si chiama Rosa. To say someone lives somewhere: I miei genitori abitano a Bari.

PART 07

Examples in context

Questa è la mia famiglia. Mio padre si chiama Paolo e mia madre si chiama Elena. Ho due fratelli e una sorella. Mio fratello maggiore è alto e ha i capelli castani. Mia sorella è molto simpatica e creativa. I miei nonni abitano in campagna. (This is my family. My father's name is Paolo and my mother's name is Elena. I have two brothers and a sister. My older brother is tall and has brown hair. My sister is very friendly and creative. My grandparents live in the countryside.)

PART 08

Practice and answer key

1. ___ madre (my mother). 2. ___ fratelli (my brothers). 3. Anna è (simpatico/simpatica). 4. Marco ha gli occhi (verde/verdi). 5. My parents live in Rome. Answers: 1. mia. 2. i miei. 3. simpatica. 4. verdi. 5. I miei genitori abitano a Roma.

PART 09

Speaking task

Describe at least three people in your family or close circle. Give their relationship to you, name, age, where they live, appearance and personality. Use at least five possessives and six descriptive adjectives.

Lesson complete

Review the examples, say the Italian phrases aloud and complete the speaking task. Return to any section that still feels difficult. Regular short practice is more effective than memorizing a long list once.
