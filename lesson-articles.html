A1 · LESSON 04 · GRAMMAR

Italian articles,
gender & number

Learn how Italian nouns work, why every noun has a grammatical gender, how singular nouns become plural, and how to choose the correct definite or indefinite article. This chapter explains the rules, the exceptions, and how to use them in real sentences.

In this chapterLearning goals1. Noun gender2. Singular & plural3. Definite articles4. Indefinite articles5. Special patterns6. Article + adjective7. Vocabulary8. Dialogue9. Practice10. Reading11. Summary

Learning goals

By the end, you should be able to identify the gender of common nouns, make nouns plural, choose the correct definite article (the) and indefinite article (a/an), and understand why forms such as lo studente, l'amica, gli amici and un'amica are different.

In Italian, the article normally agrees with the noun in gender (masculine/feminine) and number (singular/plural). English does not have this system, so you need to learn to notice it.

1. Grammatical gender: masculine or feminine

Italian nouns are classified as masculine or feminine. The classification is grammatical; it does not always describe biological sex. For example, il tavolo (table) is masculine and la sedia (chair) is feminine.

Common endings are clues

EndingTypical patternExamples
-oUsually masculineil libro (book), il ragazzo (boy), il telefono
-aUsually femininela casa (house), la ragazza, la scuola
-eCan be masculine or feminine; learn the nounil pane (bread), la notte (night), il fiore (flower)

These are tendencies, not absolute laws. A noun ending in -o is often masculine and one ending in -a is often feminine, but there are exceptions: la mano (hand) is feminine; il problema (problem) is masculine.

Learn nouns with their article

Do not memorize casa alone. Memorize la casa. The article tells you gender and helps you choose the correct forms later. Make vocabulary notes in pairs: il libro — i libri, la casa — le case.

Useful habit: Whenever you learn a new noun, record its article and plural form. This saves you from guessing later.

2. Singular and plural: how nouns change

Italian usually changes the final vowel to form the plural. The article changes too.

Singular endingPlural endingTypical genderExample
-o-iMasculinelibro → libri
-a-eFemininecasa → case
-e-iMasculine or femininefiore → fiori; notte → notti

Examples in sentences

Un ragazzo, due ragazzi.
One boy, two boys.
Una ragazza, tre ragazze.
One girl, three girls.
Un cane, due cani; una notte, due notti.
One dog, two dogs; one night, two nights.

What about nouns ending in -ca and -ga?

Many nouns keep the hard sound in the plural by adding h before e: amica → amiche (female friend), riga → righe (line/row). The h keeps the /k/ or /g/ sound before e.

Nouns ending in -cia and -gia

The plural depends on whether the i is pronounced and whether the ending is preceded by a vowel or consonant. A useful general tendency: when -cia/-gia has an i that is not stressed, it often disappears after a consonant (provincia → province); after a vowel, it often remains (camicia → camicie). There are spelling conventions and exceptions, so learn common forms as vocabulary.

Nouns that do not change

Some nouns remain the same in the plural, especially many words ending in a stressed vowel, some abbreviations and foreign words: la città → le città, il film → i film. The article or surrounding number tells you whether the noun is singular or plural.

3. Definite articles: “the”

Italian uses several forms of “the.” Choose the form by the noun's gender, number and first sound—not just its first letter.

Masculine singularFeminine singularMasculine pluralFeminine plural
Most consonantsillaile
Vowell'l'glile
Special masculine soundslo—gli—

Use LO / GLI before these masculine beginnings

  • s + consonant: lo studente, gli studenti (student)
  • z: lo zaino, gli zaini (backpack)
  • gn: lo gnomo, gli gnomi (gnome)
  • ps: lo psicologo, gli psicologi (psychologist)
  • y: lo yogurt, gli yogurt
  • Some words beginning with x or other unusual consonant groups also use lo/gli.

Full examples

SingularPluralMeaning
il libroi librithe book(s)
lo studentegli studentithe student(s)
lo zainogli zainithe backpack(s)
l'amicogli amicithe male friend(s)

Remember: Masculine plural gli is used before vowels and before the same sound groups that require lo in the singular. Feminine plural is always le.

4. Indefinite articles: “a / an”

Unlike English, Italian has no single form for “a/an.” The article agrees with gender and the beginning of the noun.

FormUseExample
unMasculine before most sounds, including vowelsun libro; un amico
unoMasculine before s + consonant, z, gn, ps, y, etc.uno studente; uno zaino
unaFeminine before consonantuna casa; una ragazza
un'Feminine before a vowelun'amica; un'idea

The apostrophe matters

Un amico is masculine and has no apostrophe. Un'amica is feminine and uses the apostrophe because una loses its final a before a vowel.

Ho un amico italiano.
I have an Italian male friend.
Ho un'amica italiana.
I have an Italian female friend.
È uno studente. È una studentessa.
He is a student. She is a student.

5. Patterns and exceptions worth learning

Feminine nouns ending in -o

La mano → le mani is a common feminine exception. Learn the whole pair.

Masculine nouns ending in -a

Many words of Greek origin ending in -ma/-ta are masculine: il problema → i problemi, il programma → i programmi, il sistema → i sistemi. Do not assume every -a noun is feminine.

Feminine nouns ending in -tà or -tù

These are usually feminine and do not change in the plural: la città → le città, la libertà → le libertà, la virtù → le virtù.

People and professions

Many professions have masculine and feminine forms: il maestro / la maestra, il professore / la professoressa, il direttore / la direttrice. Some nouns have one form for both genders and use the article to show gender: il cantante / la cantante.

Names of people vs objects

Gender is grammatical. It is not a rule that objects are masculine or feminine because of any physical characteristic. Always learn the noun's gender as part of the word.

6. Articles, nouns and adjectives work together

In Italian, the adjective often changes to agree with the noun in gender and number. The article, noun and adjective form a grammatical team.

ItalianEnglishWhat changes?
il ragazzo altothe tall boymasculine singular
i ragazzi altithe tall boysmasculine plural
la ragazza altathe tall girlfeminine singular
le ragazze altethe tall girlsfeminine plural
un libro interessantean interesting bookadjective in -e
libri interessantiinteresting books-e becomes -i in plural

Adjectives ending in -o typically have four forms (-o, -a, -i, -e). Adjectives ending in -e typically have two forms (-e singular, -i plural) for both genders. The article is a useful clue to the noun; the adjective confirms the agreement.

Why this matters

Compare un ragazzo italiano and una ragazza italiana. The noun, article and adjective change together. You cannot simply add an article and leave every other word unchanged.

8. Dialogue: in a classroom

Read first for meaning. Then read aloud and pay attention to the articles.

Giulia: Ciao! Sei una nuova studentessa?

Adam: Sì, sono uno studente. Mi chiamo Adam.

Giulia: Piacere! Io sono Giulia. Quella è la nostra aula e questi sono gli insegnanti.

Adam: Dov'è il libro di italiano?

Giulia: Il libro è sul tavolo. Gli zaini sono vicino alla porta.

Adam: Grazie! E quelle sono le amiche di Marco?

Giulia: Sì, sono le sue amiche. Sono tutte simpatiche.

English translation

Giulia: Hi! Are you a new female student?
Adam: Yes, I am a student. My name is Adam.
Giulia: Nice to meet you! I’m Giulia. That is our classroom and these are the teachers.
Adam: Where is the Italian book?
Giulia: The book is on the table. The backpacks are near the door.
Adam: Thanks! And are those Marco’s friends?
Giulia: Yes, they are his friends. They’re all nice.

Notice the forms

  • una studentessa (feminine) vs uno studente (masculine)
  • il libro (singular) vs gli zaini (plural, special masculine article)
  • le amiche (feminine plural)
  • quella and questi also agree with the noun—future lesson on demonstratives.

10. Reading: La casa di Elena

Elena ha una casa in una piccola città italiana. La casa è antica, ma le camere sono luminose. In cucina ci sono un tavolo grande e quattro sedie. Sul tavolo ci sono i libri, un quaderno e una penna. Elena ha due amici: Marco e Sara. Marco è uno studente; Sara è un'amica speciale. Gli amici visitano Elena il sabato. Portano dei fiori e una torta. La sera, le persone cenano insieme e raccontano storie.

Translation

Elena has a house in a small Italian town. The house is old, but the rooms are bright. In the kitchen there is a large table and four chairs. On the table there are books, a notebook and a pen. Elena has two friends: Marco and Sara. Marco is a student; Sara is a special friend. The friends visit Elena on Saturdays. They bring flowers and a cake. In the evening, people have dinner together and tell stories.

Comprehension

  1. Is Elena's house new or old?
  2. How many chairs are there?
  3. Who are Elena's friends?
  4. What do the friends bring?
  5. Find five definite articles and explain why each is used.

Writing and speaking task

Write 8–10 sentences about a room, your home, or a group of friends. Use at least six definite articles, four indefinite articles, three plural nouns, and two adjectives. Read your text aloud. Check each noun: article, gender, singular/plural, and adjective agreement.

11. What you should remember

  • Nouns have grammatical gender: masculine or feminine. Learn each noun together with its article.
  • Most -o nouns are masculine; most -a nouns feminine; -e nouns can be either.
  • Plural patterns are commonly -o → -i, -a → -e, -e → -i.
  • Definite articles: il / lo / l' / la; plural i / gli / le.
  • Indefinite articles: un / uno / una / un'.
  • Use lo/gli with special masculine initial sounds (s + consonant, z, gn, ps, etc.).
  • Masculine un amico has no apostrophe; feminine un'amica does.
  • Articles, nouns and adjectives agree.
