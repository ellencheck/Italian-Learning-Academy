A1 BEGINNER · GRAMMAR IN CONTEXT · LESSON 05

AVERE — to have

Learn to use avere to talk about what you have, your age, hunger, thirst, needs, and everyday experiences. Don't memorize the verb alone: practise the expressions Italians use every day.

35–50 minutesBeginner A1Grammar · vocabulary · speaking · writing
Lesson mapLearning goals1. Understand avere2. Everyday uses3. Conjugation4. Pronouns5. Expressions6. Dialogue7. Vocabulary8. Common mistakes9. Practice10. Translation11. Reading12. Your turn

BEFORE YOU START

By the end of this lesson, you can…

  • use all six present-tense forms of avere;
  • talk about possession and things you own;
  • say your age and ask someone else's age;
  • express hunger, thirst, tiredness, fear, and other common states with avere;
  • understand the difference between Italian avere expressions and English “to be” expressions;
  • ask and answer everyday questions naturally.

Study method: Say the Italian aloud. Repeat each expression as a whole phrase, not word by word. Cover the translation and try to recall it; then use it in a sentence about your real life.

01 · CORE IDEA

What does avere mean?

Avere is the Italian verb “to have.” It can express possession, relationships, age, and many physical or emotional states. English and Italian don't always use the same verb: in English we say “I am hungry,” but Italian literally says “I have hunger.”

EnglishItalianLiteral idea
I have a book.Ho un libro.I have a book.
She is 25.Lei ha 25 anni.She has 25 years.
We are hungry.Abbiamo fame.We have hunger.
They are afraid.Hanno paura.They have fear.
Key idea:

When Italian uses avere for a state or sensation, learn the whole expression. Don't translate English “am” automatically as sono.

02 · FOUR EVERYDAY USES

When do you use avere?

A. Possession and relationships

Use avere to say what you own, carry, or have available—and to talk about family members or other relationships.

Ho una macchina. Hai un fratello? Abbiamo due amici italiani.
I have a car. Do you have a brother? We have two Italian friends.
The noun follows the conjugated verb. A question can be made with intonation and a question mark; the word order stays simple.

B. Age

Italian uses avere + number + anni to express age. You don't use essere for age.

Ho trent'anni. Quanti anni hai? Mia sorella ha diciotto anni.
I'm thirty. How old are you? My sister is eighteen.
Literally: “I have thirty years.” The expression anni is normally included.

C. Physical sensations and needs

Many basic sensations use avere rather than essere. These expressions are very common in conversation.

Ho fame e ho sete. Hai sonno? Abbiamo freddo.
I'm hungry and thirsty. Are you sleepy? We're cold.
Fame, sete, sonno, freddo and caldo are nouns in these expressions, so Italian uses avere.

D. Emotions, conditions and needs

Some expressions with avere describe fear, urgency, desire, or a need to do something. Learn them as useful chunks.

Ho paura. Hai fretta? Hanno bisogno di riposare.
I'm afraid. Are you in a hurry? They need to rest.
Avere bisogno di means “to need.” The following noun or infinitive comes after di.

04 · SUBJECT PRONOUNS

Do you always need io, tu, or noi?

No. Italian usually drops subject pronouns because the verb form identifies the person. You can say Ho un cane rather than Io ho un cane. Keep the pronoun for emphasis, contrast, or clarity.

With pronounNatural sentenceMeaning
Io ho due sorelle.Ho due sorelle.I have two sisters.
Tu hai fame?Hai fame?Are you hungry?
Noi abbiamo tempo.Abbiamo tempo.We have time.
Loro hanno una casa.Hanno una casa.They have a house.

Formal “you”

In polite conversation, Lei (capitalized) takes the third-person singular form ha: Lei ha una prenotazione? — Do you have a reservation? Context tells you that Lei is formal “you,” not “she.”

05 · EXPRESSIONS TO MEMORIZE

Italian uses avere where English uses “to be”

These are high-frequency expressions. Learn the Italian phrase and its English meaning together; literal translation can sound strange, but the Italian expression is completely natural.

Italian expressionEnglish meaningExample
avere fameto be hungryHo fame.
avere seteto be thirstyHai sete?
avere sonnoto be sleepyHo sonno.
avere caldoto feel hotAbbiamo caldo.
avere freddoto feel coldHai freddo?
avere paurato be afraidHa paura.
avere frettato be in a hurryHo fretta.
avere ragioneto be rightHai ragione.
avere tortoto be wrongHo torto.
avere bisogno dito needAbbiamo bisogno di aiuto.
avere voglia dito feel like / want toHo voglia di dormire.
avere … annito be … years oldHo 22 anni.
How to build sentences:

Conjugated avere + expression. For example, ho fame (I'm hungry), hai fame (you're hungry), ha fame (he/she is hungry). Change only the verb form to match the subject.

Need + infinitive

With avere bisogno di, use di before a noun or infinitive: Ho bisogno di acqua (I need water); Ho bisogno di dormire (I need to sleep). With avere voglia di, the same pattern expresses desire: Hai voglia di uscire? (Do you feel like going out?).

06 · LISTEN WITH YOUR VOICE

Dialogue: at a café

Read once for the meaning, a second time out loud, and a third time with the translations covered. Pay attention to the questions using avere.

BaristaBuongiorno! Che cosa desidera?
Good morning! What would you like?
ElenaUn cappuccino, grazie. Avete anche tè?
A cappuccino, please. Do you also have tea?
BaristaSì, abbiamo tè verde e tè nero.
Yes, we have green tea and black tea.
ElenaPerfetto. Ho un po' di freddo oggi.
Perfect. I'm a little cold today.
BaristaVuole qualcosa da mangiare? Abbiamo cornetti.
Would you like something to eat? We have croissants.
ElenaSì, grazie. Ho anche fame!
Yes, thank you. I'm hungry too!
BaristaAllora, un cappuccino e un cornetto. Ha bisogno di altro?
So, a cappuccino and a croissant. Do you need anything else?
ElenaNo, basta così. Quanto ho da pagare?
No, that's all. How much do I have to pay?

Dialogue breakdown

Avete anche tè? · Abbiamo tè verde.
Do you have tea too? · We have green tea.
Avete asks a plural “you” (the café/staff). Abbiamo means “we have.”
Ho un po' di freddo. · Ho fame.
I'm a little cold. · I'm hungry.
Both are fixed everyday expressions with avere.
Ha bisogno di altro?
Do you need anything else?
The barista uses formal Lei; ha is the third-person singular form.

Useful expressions

08 · COMMON MISTAKES

Four mistakes to avoid

1. Using essere for age

❌ Sono 20 anni. → ✅ Ho 20 anni.
I'm 20 years old.
Age is expressed with avere + number + anni.

2. Translating “I'm hungry” word for word

❌ Sono fame. → ✅ Ho fame.
I'm hungry.
Fame is a noun (“hunger”), and Italian uses avere with it.

3. Forgetting silent H

❌ o una macchina → ✅ Ho una macchina.
I have a car.
The H is silent, but it must be written in the verb form ho.

4. Confusing hanno and anno

Hanno due cani. / L'anno è lungo.
They have two dogs. / The year is long.
Hanno (they have) and anno (year) sound alike; context and spelling distinguish them.
Accent check:

Unlike è (is), the avere forms ho, hai, ha, hanno use H but no accent. À with accent is not the standard form of this verb.

09 · GUIDED PRACTICE

Choose the correct form

Try each one before revealing the answer.

1. Io ___ una sorella. (ho / hai)

Ho una sorella. — I have a sister.

2. Tu ___ fame? (ha / hai)

Hai fame? — Are you hungry?

3. Marco ___ ventidue anni. (ha / hanno)

Marco ha ventidue anni. — Marco is 22.

4. Noi ___ bisogno di acqua. (abbiamo / avete)

Abbiamo bisogno di acqua. — We need water.

5. Voi ___ una prenotazione? (hanno / avete)

Avete una prenotazione? — Do you have a reservation?

6. Loro ___ paura. (ha / hanno)

Hanno paura. — They are afraid.

7. Lei ___ una macchina? (ha / ho)

Lei ha una macchina? — Does she have a car? (or formal: Do you have a car?)

11 · READING FOR MEANING

Una giornata al mare

Read the story once without translating every word. Look for the people, their age, what they have, and how they feel.

Oggi Anna e Luca hanno una giornata libera. Anna ha ventotto anni e Luca ne ha trenta. Hanno una macchina piccola, ma hanno tutto quello che serve per andare al mare: acqua, panini, un ombrellone e due asciugamani. Anna ha fame, Luca ha sete. “Hai anche il caffè?” chiede Anna. “Sì, ho due thermos!” risponde Luca. Al mare hanno caldo, ma sono felici. Nel pomeriggio hanno voglia di fare una passeggiata. Non hanno fretta: oggi hanno molto tempo.

Today Anna and Luca have a free day. Anna is 28 and Luca is 30. They have a small car, but they have everything they need to go to the beach: water, sandwiches, a beach umbrella and two towels. Anna is hungry, Luca is thirsty. “Do you also have coffee?” Anna asks. “Yes, I have two thermoses!” Luca answers. At the beach they're hot, but happy. In the afternoon they feel like taking a walk. They're not in a hurry: today they have lots of time.

Comprehension

  1. How old are Anna and Luca?
  2. What things do they take to the beach?
  3. Who is hungry? Who is thirsty?
  4. Do they have coffee?
  5. Are they in a hurry?
1. Anna is 28 and Luca is 30. 2. Water, sandwiches, an umbrella and two towels. 3. Anna is hungry; Luca is thirsty. 4. Yes, Luca has two thermoses. 5. No, they have plenty of time.

12 · MAKE IT PERSONAL

Talk about what you have and how you feel

Write 6–8 sentences about yourself. Include your age (if you want), possessions, family, and two expressions with avere. Use the model to get started, then make it personal.

Ho ______ anni. Ho ______. Ho / non ho ______. Oggi ho fame / sete / sonno. Ho bisogno di ______. Ho voglia di ______. La mia famiglia ha ______.

I am ___ years old. I have ___. I have / don't have ___. Today I'm hungry / thirsty / sleepy. I need ___. I feel like ___. My family has ___.

Speaking challenge

Imagine you're meeting a new friend. Tell them your age, whether you have brothers or sisters, what you have in your bag today, and how you feel. Ask them at least three questions with hai: Hai fame? Hai fratelli? Hai tempo? Try speaking without reading.

LESSON CHECKPOINT

What should you remember?

  • Avere means “to have,” but Italian also uses it for age and many sensations.
  • Forms: ho · hai · ha · abbiamo · avete · hanno.
  • The H is silent but essential in spelling: ho, hai, ha, hanno.
  • Age uses avere: Ho 30 anni.
  • Learn fixed expressions: avere fame, sete, sonno, caldo, freddo, paura, fretta, bisogno di.
