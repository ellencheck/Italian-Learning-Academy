Places, Prepositions & Directions

Find your way around an Italian town, describe locations and ask for or give simple directions.

Learning goals

You will name common places, ask where something is, use prepositions of place, understand simple directions and explain a short route.

PART 06

C'è and ci sono

C'è means “there is” and is followed by a singular noun. Ci sono means “there are” and is followed by a plural noun.

SingularPlural
C'è una banca.Ci sono due banche.
C'è un bar.Ci sono tre bar.
C'è una fermata.Ci sono molte fermate.
Non c'è una farmacia qui. Non ci sono taxi.
There isn't a pharmacy here. There are no taxis.

PART 07

Dialogue: asking for the station

TuristaScusi, dov'è la stazione?
Excuse me, where is the station?
PassanteVada sempre dritto, poi giri a sinistra al semaforo.
Go straight ahead, then turn left at the traffic light.
TuristaÈ lontana?
Is it far?
PassanteNo, sono cinque minuti a piedi.
No, it's five minutes on foot.
TuristaGrazie mille!
Thank you very much!

PART 08

Build a route

Use a sequence of short instructions. Start with a landmark, then give one action at a time.

Dalla piazza, vai dritto. Gira a destra. La farmacia è accanto alla banca.
From the square, go straight. Turn right. The pharmacy is next to the bank.
Useful connector

Use poi (then), dopo (afterwards) and alla fine (finally) to make directions clear.

PART 09

Practice and answer key

  1. There is a museum.
  2. There are two cafés.
  3. Turn right (formal).
  4. The bank is opposite the station.
  5. Where is the pharmacy?
Answers

1. C'è un museo. 2. Ci sono due bar. 3. Giri a destra. 4. La banca è di fronte alla stazione. 5. Dov'è la farmacia?

PART 10

Speaking assignment

Imagine a visitor is standing in your town square. Explain how to get to the station, a café and a pharmacy. Use six direction phrases, three prepositions and both c'è and ci sono.

Lesson complete

Review the examples, say the Italian phrases aloud and complete the speaking task. Return to any section that still feels difficult. Regular short practice is more effective than memorizing a long list once.
